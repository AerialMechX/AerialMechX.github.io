Sim-to-real latent world models for a tethered tilt-rotor aerial robot

ETH ZürichRobotics Student Fellowship, Autonomous Systems Lab, Jul–Aug 2026

Can a world model trained only in simulation capture the aerodynamic, actuator, and tether effects of a real aerial robot, and still run fast enough for onboard control?

  1. Problem

    Real flights involve aerodynamic effects and external disturbances that are hard to model precisely. On this omnidirectional tilt-rotor platform, the tether adds forces and moments that change during flight.

  2. Approach

    Train a latent world model only on domain-randomized simulation data, so it learns a compact representation of the robot’s dynamics and predicts how the system evolves. Probe that representation for physical quantities: velocity, attitude, actuator behavior, mass, inertia, and external force and torque. For onboard use, a compact fixed-history MLP trained with a JEPA-style objective plus a residual force-and-torque objective.

  3. Result

    The model transferred to unseen real-flight data. Probing showed that reliable estimation of physical parameters depends on sufficiently informative training motions. The compact model improved disturbance estimation on real-flight data and cut inference time from about 3–4 ms to 0.7 ms.

AeroGrab: autonomous aerial grasping in cluttered environments

IROS 2026First authorRobotics Research Center, IIIT Hyderabad

Problem
Picking a specified object out of clutter from the air: targets are often occluded, and grasps must be stable and reachable for an arm mounted on a hovering vehicle.
Approach
One framework that couples language-specified target understanding, active perception, 6-DoF grasp generation, motion planning, and closed-loop manipulation. A lightweight hybrid CNN–ViT predicts 6-DoF grasps in under 38 ms, a hierarchical risk-aware decision layer filters them for kinematic reachability, and SAM 3-based tracking with orbital search maneuvers resolves occlusions.
My role
First author. Led the system design, integration, and experiments.
Result
End-to-end autonomous aerial grasping in clutter, from a language-specified target to grasp execution.

Whole-body reinforcement learning with an inner-loop dynamics estimator for aerial manipulation

IEEE CASE 2026First authorRobotics Research Center, IIIT Hyderabad

Problem
Policies trained in simulation degrade on hardware when payload and vehicle–arm coupling differ from training, and in aerial manipulation both change every time the arm moves or grasps.
Approach
Hierarchical control. A whole-body PPO policy, trained with randomized payload mass, inertia, and actuator properties, outputs thrust and body-rate commands. An adaptive INDI inner loop at 250 Hz updates its control-effectiveness estimate online from filtered angular-acceleration feedback and rejects disturbances during manipulation.
My role
First author. Designed the hierarchical architecture and training setup, and ran the hardware experiments.
Result
Validated in hardware on a 3-DoF aerial manipulator, including grasp-and-drop tasks with payload changes.

Learning the non-stationary dynamics of aerial manipulators

IFAC World Congress 2026Co-first authorRobotics Research Center, IIIT Hyderabad

Problem
An aerial manipulator’s dynamics shift abruptly when it picks up a payload or reconfigures its arm, so a single fixed model fails exactly when accuracy matters most.
Approach
AERMANI-Diffusion, a regime-conditioned diffusion model of the robot’s residual dynamics that captures abrupt payload and configuration changes, used to improve prediction and closed-loop tracking.
My role
Co-first author, with equal contribution from Samaksh Ujjawal.
Result
Better dynamics prediction and closed-loop tracking in real-flight tests, including high-acceleration maneuvers.

Related, at IROS 2026: Learn Structure, Adapt on the Fly, multi-scale residual learning with lightweight online adaptation to unseen payloads.

Sampling-based planning with real-time MPC for agile quadrotor flight

2025

RRT finds collision-free routes, a trajectory module time-parameterizes them for aggressive flight, and a constrained MPC tracks them with thrust and body-rate commands. Warm starts and a simplified model keep the optimization real-time.

100 Hz closed-loop MPC on hardware

Autonomous ball catcher

2025

A mobile robot that predicts where a thrown ball will land and drives there first. External cameras triangulate the ball, a physics-based fit with RANSAC predicts the landing point, and targets stream over UDP to a PID-controlled differential drive.

30 Hz target streaming, Raspberry Pi and Arduino

Research interests

My research asks how aerial robots can act reliably when their dynamics change mid-task: when they grasp a payload, make contact, or fly through disturbances that no first-principles model fully captures. I pair learned models with control structure and test the result on hardware.

Dynamics learning and world models

Latent, residual, and diffusion-based models of non-stationary dynamics that transfer from simulation to real flight, and representations that expose physically meaningful quantities.

World models at ASL, AERMANI-Diffusion

Learning with control-theoretic structure

Reinforcement learning and learned models combined with adaptive, INDI, impedance, and predictive control, so that learned components stay reliable under model mismatch.

RL with an inner-loop estimator, impedance adaptation (ICCAS 2025)

Perception-driven aerial manipulation

Active perception and 6-DoF grasping for aerial manipulators working in clutter.

AeroGrab

Longer term, I want aerial robots that can physically intervene in hazardous, unstructured environments, such as inspection sites and disaster zones, so that people don’t have to.

In a PhD, I want to work on world models that support control under distribution shift, fast online adaptation, and contact-rich aerial manipulation.
